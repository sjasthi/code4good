# Web Application Project: Common Themes

**Learn and Help | Code4Good | Middle and High School Students**

A web application helps people complete tasks through a web browser. Use these 32 planning questions, grouped into seven categories, to guide your course project.

Discuss each item with your team. Check it off when you have made a decision or verified it. Some features may not apply to your app; explain why when you skip one.

## 1. Purpose, People, and Impact

- [ ] **1. Purpose:** What problem does your app solve? How will it help people?
- [ ] **2. Stakeholders:** Who uses, supports, manages, or benefits from the app? Examples include students, parents, volunteers, and community organizations.
- [ ] **3. Success and community impact:** How will you know your app works and helps its intended community? Choose a simple measure, such as successful volunteer sign-ups.

## 2. Project Planning and Teamwork

- [ ] **4. Main features:** What must users be able to accomplish? Decide which features are essential and which can wait.
- [ ] **5. Project scope and milestones:** What can your team realistically finish? Set small goals for planning, building, testing, and presenting.
- [ ] **6. Wireframes and prototypes:** Sketch the main screens and try out the layout before writing code. A wireframe is a simple drawing of a screen.
- [ ] **7. Teamwork and documentation:** Who handles each task? How will you track changes and explain how to set up and use the app?

## 3. User Roles, Access, and App Rules

- [ ] **8. User roles:** What types of users will you have—for example, visitors, members, volunteers, and administrators?
- [ ] **9. Permissions and CRUD:** For each type of information, decide who can Create (add), Read (view), Update (edit), and Delete (remove) it. What should each role be prevented from doing?
- [ ] **10. Accounts and sign-in:** Do users need accounts? How will they sign in, sign out, and recover access?
- [ ] **11. Rules and workflows:** What rules must the app follow? For example, prevent duplicate registrations and stop sign-ups when an event is full.

## 4. User Experience and Interaction

- [ ] **12. User journeys:** What steps does someone follow to complete a task, such as registering for an event?
- [ ] **13. Pages and navigation:** What pages do you need? Are menus, links, and buttons consistent and easy to find?
- [ ] **14. Forms and input validation:** Are fields clearly labeled? Does the app check entries and explain how to fix mistakes?
- [ ] **15. Feedback and messages:** Does the app clearly show when something succeeds, fails, is loading, or has no results?
- [ ] **16. Mistake prevention and recovery:** Should users confirm a deletion? Can they cancel, undo, or correct an action?
- [ ] **17. Search, sorting, and filtering:** How will users find the information they need? Include these tools when they help users.

## 5. Visual Design, Content, and Inclusion

- [ ] **18. Branding:** Do the app's name, logo, colors, fonts, and tone fit its mission?
- [ ] **19. Hero section:** Does the top of the homepage clearly explain the app and provide a main action, such as “Volunteer” or “Explore Projects”?
- [ ] **20. Consistent design:** Do headings, buttons, forms, spacing, and page layouts look and behave consistently?
- [ ] **21. Responsiveness:** Does the app work well on phones, tablets, and computers?
- [ ] **22. Accessibility:** Can people with different abilities use it? Consider readable text, color contrast, keyboard navigation, and image descriptions.
- [ ] **23. Content:** Is the information clear, accurate, appropriate, and easy to understand?
- [ ] **24. Responsible use of content:** Do you have permission to use your images, text, and code? Give credit to the original creators and follow any usage conditions.

## 6. Data, Security, and Privacy

- [ ] **25. Data and storage:** What information must the app save? How is it organized and connected?
- [ ] **26. Security:** How will you protect accounts and data? The server must check permissions before allowing an action; hiding a button alone does not protect data.
- [ ] **27. Privacy:** What personal information do you really need? Who can see it? Use fictional data while developing.

## 7. Reliability, Testing, and Improvement

- [ ] **28. Outside services:** Will your app use maps, videos, or other services? What happens if those services are unavailable?
- [ ] **29. Performance:** Do pages load quickly? Are images appropriately sized?
- [ ] **30. Testing:** Have you checked different roles, screen sizes, browsers, and both valid and invalid inputs?
- [ ] **31. Hosting and maintenance:** Where will the app run? Who will fix problems, update content, and back up data?
- [ ] **32. User feedback and improvement:** Can someone outside your team try the app? Observe where they get confused and use their feedback to improve it.
