# Code For Good - Week 8 Outline

## 📅 Week 8 Overview

**Date:** Sunday, November 8, 2026

Welcome to Week 8! Every student is building a web application for their own final project. Although our ideas are different, our applications share many common themes: a clear purpose, people who use them, rules about what users can do, useful pages, consistent design, and careful handling of data.

**Goal:** Turn your project idea into a clear, realistic project specification—a written plan describing what your application will do and how people will use it.

## 📚 Topics Covered Today

### [1] Web Application Development: Common Themes
**Different projects, shared planning questions**

📊 [Reference: Web Application Development: Common Themes](https://github.com/sjasthi/code4good/blob/main/Presentations/web_app_project_common_themes.md)

Explore the **32 planning questions in seven categories**:

1. **Purpose, People, and Impact:** What problem are you solving, and who benefits?
2. **Project Planning and Teamwork:** What features can you finish, and what are your milestones?
3. **User Roles, Access, and App Rules:** Who can do what?
4. **User Experience and Interaction:** How do people navigate, enter information, and complete tasks?
5. **Visual Design, Content, and Inclusion:** How do branding, the hero section, responsiveness, and accessibility help users?
6. **Data, Security, and Privacy:** What information do you store, and how do you protect it?
7. **Reliability, Testing, and Improvement:** How will you test, publish, maintain, and improve your application?

**Key Learning Objectives:**

- Identify stakeholders: people who use, support, manage, or benefit from your app.
- Distinguish user roles from permissions.
- Explain **CRUD**: Create (add), Read (view), Update (edit), and Delete (remove).
- Plan consistent navigation, clear forms, and helpful success/error messages.
- Consider phone and desktop layouts, accessibility, security, and privacy.
- Choose a small set of essential features for your first working version.

> Not every planning question requires a new feature. If something does not apply to your project, explain why.

### [2] Project Specification: An Example (Scholarships Project)
**See how an idea becomes a written plan**

📋 [Reference: Pathways to Scholarships — Project Specification](https://github.com/sjasthi/ICS325-Web-Application-Development/blob/main/Final-Project/ics325_scholarships_requirements.md)

**What to Notice:**

- The problem, intended users, and purpose are explained first.
- Roles such as visitor, student, parent, administrator, and super-admin have different permissions.
- Features describe tasks users need to complete.
- Pages, data, security, testing, and milestones support those features.
- Work is divided into smaller iterations, each with a clear outcome.

**Discussion:** Which parts of this example would help you describe your own project?

> This is a college-level example for inspiration. Your Code4Good project can be much smaller. Adapt the structure and level of detail to your idea; the example's technology stack, five roles, and ten iterations are not requirements for your project.

### [5] AI Prompts for Your Project Idea
**Use examples to make your idea clearer**

Use these **three prompts in order** with GitHub Copilot or another instructor-approved AI tool. Replace the text in square brackets with your own information.

**Before you start:** Provide the Common Themes document from **[1]** as a checklist and the Scholarships Project Specification from **[2]** as a worked example. Paste or attach the documents if the AI cannot open the links. One worked example is called **one-shot prompting**; adding a second short, instructor-approved project specification makes it **few-shot prompting**.

#### Prompt 1: Elaborate My Idea

```text
Act as a project planning coach for a middle or high school Code4Good student.

My web application idea is: [describe your idea].
The community I want to help is: [describe the people].
My current skills and available time are: [describe them].

Use the Common Themes document as a checklist and the Scholarships Project Specification as one worked example. Adapt the example to my idea and experience level.

Ask me up to five focused questions about anything important that is missing. Wait for my answers. Then help me write a short project overview covering the problem, intended users, community benefit, and three to five essential features. Separate optional future features from the first version. Use plain language and do not write code yet.
```

#### Prompt 2: Draft My Project Specification

```text
Using my answers and project overview, draft a beginner-friendly specification for my web application: [project name and updated idea].

Use the Common Themes document and the Scholarships Project Specification I provided as references. Borrow their useful organization while keeping the scope realistic for my Code4Good project.

Include:
- Purpose, stakeholders, and a simple measure of success.
- User roles and a table showing who can Create, Read, Update, and Delete each main type of information, including actions they must not perform.
- Essential features, main pages, consistent navigation, and one step-by-step user journey.
- Branding, homepage hero section, main action, phone/desktop layout, and accessibility.
- Data to store, form validation, security, and privacy.
- Three small milestones and a short testing checklist.

Explain whether accounts are actually needed. Mark unknown details as questions instead of inventing them. Use fictional sample data. Do not automatically copy the scholarship project's technology stack or features, and do not write code yet.
```

#### Prompt 3: Review and Improve My Plan

```text
Review the project specification below against the Common Themes checklist and the structure of the Scholarships Project Specification.

My specification:
[paste your latest draft]

Identify unclear requirements, missing permissions, privacy concerns, confusing navigation, and features that may be too large for my available time. For each issue, explain why it matters and suggest a simple improvement. Identify checklist items that do not apply and explain why.

Then recommend the smallest useful first version and write five testable acceptance criteria using "Given / When / Then" in plain language. Include a normal user task, invalid input, an action a role must not perform, a phone layout check, and a keyboard accessibility check. Ask me to decide any unresolved choices before producing a revised specification. Do not write code yet.
```

**Remember:** Read the AI's suggestions, correct assumptions, and choose features you understand and can explain. Do not paste passwords or private personal information into prompts.

## 🎮 Practice & Review

### Hands-on Practice: Plan Your Own Application

1. Explain your project idea in two or three sentences.
2. Review the Common Themes checklist and identify decisions you still need to make.
3. Use the three AI prompts to explore, draft, and review your plan.
4. Personalize the specification so it reflects your own decisions.
5. Sketch the homepage and one important task screen.
6. Share your first-version features and one question with the class.

**Discussion Prompts:**

- Who benefits from your app, and what task will they complete?
- What can a visitor do? What requires another role?
- Which information can each role view or change?
- What is the smallest useful version you can build?
- How will you know a feature works?
